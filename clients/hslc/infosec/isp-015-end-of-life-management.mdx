## Intent

The purpose of this policy is to establish a strategy, philosophy, and goal for tracking, planning, and replacing end of life (EOL) hardware and software in a timely fashion to protect the bank's network from threats.

## Scope

The scope of this policy covers all technology related assets that receive support updates and in which the primary intent of the updates is to increase security, patch vulnerabilities, and/or sustain the functionality of the hardware or software.

## Policy

Because HSLC acknowledges that maintaining assets which are viable and capable of receiving patches, updates, and support is a vital control to mitigate risk and an important principle of the information security program, HSLC establishes the following:

- EOL expected dates for hardware and software assets shall be tracked and documented whenever an end of life date is published by the software or hardware manufacturer or is identified by other means. IT department are responsible for notifying the ITSC and updating EOL documentation.

- When fiscal and practical concerns allow, the bank will endeavor to adequately plan for replacements or upgrades of EOL assets in advance of the EOL dates to facilitate replacement of the asset before the EOL date occurs.

- When the bank becomes aware that any assets will not be replaced before EOL dates, the bank will discuss the risk of using the assets that are approaching EOL. The bank may also discuss mitigating controls that may be implemented to lessen the risk of the EOL asset to an acceptable level. The bank retains the right to accept those risks pursuant to the risk discussion. The risk determination will be done using the existing IT risk assessment worksheets and process for assessing risk for IT assets. The discussions will take place during the IT Steering Committee meeting.

- The IT department will perform proactive monitoring and maintenance processes and review quarterly scanning and monitoring reports that help identify any EOL vulnerabilities. This report will be presented to the ITSC identifying all low, medium and high-risk vulnerabilities to insure that EOL tracking documentation is accurate and that assets which are EOL or approaching EOL have a remediation plan.

- The bank personnel and, in particular the IT department, may need to periodically contact service providers or hardware/software manufacturers to receive information about EOL projections, concerns, and options for replacement.

- It is the responsibility of the IT department to maintain an accurate inventory of all organizational assets.

## Compliance

The ITSC is responsible for implementing this policy, insuring compliance, and will provide oversight by periodically reviewing EOL documentation, software and hardware inventory, and IT project plans to ensure that EOL assets are identified and included in budgetary planning that will result whenever possible in timely replacement.
