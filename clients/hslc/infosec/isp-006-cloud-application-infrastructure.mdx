## Intent

The purpose of this policy is to identify the controls in place to protect the confidentiality, integrity and availability of HSLC's information systems as they relate to cloud computing environments and services. This policy outlines the best practices and operational procedures for the use of cloud computing services for the processing, sharing, storage, and management of the bank's data.

## Scope

Cloud computing is defined as the use of hosted services, such as data storage, servers, databases, networking, and software over the internet, without any direct, active management by the end user. The scope of this policy includes all cloud computing services, and the users of these services, including employees, service providers, contractors, consultants, vendors, and any other party who provides services to the bank, or interacts with the bank's network and/or information systems. This policy applies to all types of cloud computing environments, including Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS), Platform as a Service (PaaS), and Software as a Service (SaaS).

## Policy

### General

- The ITSC will oversee, and approve, the procurement of cloud computing services. Cloud service provider relationships will be subject to, and managed by, the guidelines set forth in the bank's Service Provider Selection and Management Policy and through the bank's overall risk management program.

- Employees are not authorized to enter into any agreement associated with cloud computing service providers without prior written authorization from the ITSC.

- The use of cloud computing services must comply with all other provisions of this Information Security Policy, the Acceptable Use Policy, and all of the other existing information security related policies in place for the bank.

### Identity & Access Management

- Access to cloud resources will be granted based on the principle of least privilege, ensuring that users have the minimum access necessary to perform their job functions. Duties will be segregated and restrict access to sensitive data.

- All users must use strong, unique passwords and, where possible, multi-factor authentication (MFA) to access cloud services.

- Access to cloud services will be monitored, and any unauthorized access or anomalies should be reported to the IT department immediately.

### Risk Management

- Cloud computing is exposed to the same types of threats, vulnerabilities, and risks as other technology environments, whether it is managed internally or at a third party service provider. Although exposed to the same risks, cloud computing may require different security configurations and processes, so it is necessary for the bank to evaluate the risks and potential additional controls in the use of cloud computing services.

- Tenancy is one of the unique risk considerations when dealing with cloud computing technologies, and each cloud computing relationship will be with a single or multi-tenant architecture. When the bank is determining whether or not to enter into a cloud computing relationship, the following benefits and risks will be evaluated:

  - Improved security but higher cost with a single tenant, compared to a more cost-effective multi-tenant solution with added risks, such as from multiple users accessing a single platform

  - Customization of configurations with a single tenant, compared to limited customization with a multi-tenant solution

  - More flexibility with adding services and resources with multi-tenant solutions, compared with limited scalability of single tenant solutions

- Cloud computing threats, vulnerabilities, risks, and controls are addressed extensively in the bank's GLBA IT Risk Assessment.

### Data Protection, Ownership, and Privacy

- All sensitive data stored, processed, or transmitted in the cloud shall be encrypted both in transit and at rest using strong encryption algorithms that meet the bank's data protection standards.

- The bank retains full ownership of all data hosted in the cloud and must ensure that cloud service providers do not have any rights to use, modify, or disclose the bank's data without prior authorization. Additionally, the bank will maintain control over its encryption keys or use trusted key management services.

- The storage and processing of personal data in the cloud must comply with all applicable privacy regulations.

- Data retention policies must be defined and enforced to ensure data is stored only for as long as necessary for business or legal purposes.

- Data backup processes, whether internally or at the cloud computing provider, shall be in place for all critical data stored, processed, or transmitted by a cloud computing provider. Backup processes shall be reviewed during periodic reviews of the service providers and during the bank's routine monitoring and maintenance processes.

### Responsibility

- In cloud computing relationships, responsibility will be shared by the bank and the cloud computing service provider.

- Regardless of the service model type, the bank or the service provider will be responsible for the security of the assets under their direct control. For example:

  - Cloud providers will be responsible for the physical security layer such as all hardware and infrastructure, the virtualization layer, network controls and services they provide, and the facilities that run the cloud resources.

  - The bank and its employees or end users will be responsible for IAM processes, internal technical controls (endpoint security, network security), and configurations on internal systems.

- When executing a new contract with a cloud service provider, it should clearly define the responsibilities of all parties involved.

- For ongoing cloud computing relationships, employees are responsible for reporting any known incidents or performance issues with cloud services to their direct supervisor.

- Employees are responsible for safeguarding their login credentials and ensuring they are not shared with unauthorized individuals.

- Employees shall not store or transmit sensitive information using unauthorized cloud services or applications.

## Compliance and Ongoing Monitoring

Ongoing monitoring will take place for all cloud computing service providers in accordance with the bank's Service Provider Management Policy and through its routine monitoring and maintenance processes to detect and respond to security threats, vulnerabilities, and anomalies in cloud environments. Cloud security configurations shall be regularly reviewed and updated to protect against emerging threats, and the bank shall ensure the cloud environments are regularly audited for compliance with internal security policies, regulatory requirements, and industry standards. Any identified vulnerabilities or incidents shall be documented and reported to the ITSC and remediated in a timely manner.
