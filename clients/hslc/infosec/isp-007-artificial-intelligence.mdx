## Intent

The purpose of this policy is to establish guidelines for the ethical, secure, effective, and compliant use of artificial intelligence (AI) technologies, and to identify the controls in place for AI systems to protect the confidentiality, integrity and availability of HSLC's information systems. It aims to ensure AI technologies are used in a manner that aligns with the bank's values, risk management program, customer trust, and complies with all applicable laws and regulations.

## Scope

This policy applies to all employees, contractors, vendors, and third-party service providers involved in the development, procurement, deployment, general use of, or oversight of AI systems across the bank's operations, including but not limited to:

- Lending and credit risk assessment
- Fraud detection
- Customer service
- Marketing personalization
- Compliance monitoring
- All other internal operational use

## Definitions

- Artificial Intelligence (AI): Technologies that perform tasks requiring human-like intelligence, including machine learning, natural language processing, and predictive analytics.

- Machine Learning (ML): A subset of AI where algorithms improve from data over time.

- Automated Decision-Making (ADM): Systems making decisions with minimal human intervention.

## Policy

### Responsibilities, Governance, and Oversight

- IT Steering Committee
  - Review and approve all AI models for use and oversee the technical implementation and security of the models.
  - Ensure regulatory and ethical compliance in for all users involved with the use of AI
  - Ensure proactive monitoring and maintenance of AI systems is conducted

- IT Department
  - Securely implement AI models.
  - Conduct proactive monitoring and maintenance of all AI systems in use.

- Employees and Other Users
  - Document the purpose, data inputs, outcomes, and risks of the AI model in use.
  - Adhere to all applicable laws such as fair lending, privacy, and consumer protection laws.
  - Ensure the use of AI complies with all other policies of the bank.

### Regulatory Compliance

The bank will ensure all AI systems adhere to all relevant rules and regulations such as:

- Data privacy laws (e.g., GDPR, CCPA).

- Financial regulation agencies (e.g. OCC, FDIC, and CFPB) and consumer protection laws (e.g. GLBA, FCRA, TILA, FHA).

### Development & Deployment Standards

- Only high-quality, relevant, and representative data may be used for model training. Data lineage, labeling, and anonymization must be documented.

- AI models must undergo testing, validation, and approval prior to deployment. Models must be retrained and monitored regularly for concept drift or bias.

- Each AI model must include the following elements:
  - Objectives and intended use.
  - Training data and features used.
  - Performance metrics and testing results.
  - Mitigation plans for failure or unintended outcomes.

### Risk Management

Artificial intelligence is exposed to the same types of threats, vulnerabilities, and risks as other technology environments, whether it is managed internally or at a third-party service provider. Although exposed to the same risks, AI behaves inherently different than other applications or processes, so it is necessary for the bank to evaluate how AI systems operate and perform additional steps in assessing risks. For example:

- AI models must be validated for accuracy, robustness, and performance over time.

- The bank should maintain a model inventory with versioning, change logs, and validation history.

- Conduct bias and disparate impact testing for models affecting credit decisions.

- Ensure AI decision can be reasonably explained to consumers and regulators.

When evaluating the risk of AI, it is necessary to distinguish between the different types and use cases for AI. The level of risk management for evaluation, regular use, and ongoing monitoring for each of the tiers defined in the table below is based on the model's risk to the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of the information being used and processed. Data classification for assets and information is defined in the bank's GLBA IT Risk Assessment.

| Tier | Description | Examples | Governance Requirements |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tier 1 (High Risk) | Models with significant financial, legal, or reputational impact. | Credit underwriting, AML detection, fraud scoring, loan default prediction. | Full documentation, independent validation, regular monitoring, and board-level reporting. |
| Tier 2 (Medium Risk) | Models with moderate impact or used to support decisions. | Marketing segmentation, churn prediction, product recommendation engines. | Documented testing, periodic review, business line approval. |
| Tier 3 (Low Risk) | Models with limited or internal-only impact, used for automation or analytics. | Internal workflow automation, staff productivity tools. | Basic documentation, business sign-off, light monitoring. |

### Service Provider Management

AI-related service providers shall be reviewed to ensure compliance with this policy. Service providers must provide documentation on model design, bias testing, and risk controls. All third-party models must undergo internal validation before deployment. Contracts must include clauses ensuring transparency, audit rights, and risk mitigation protocols. See the bank's Service Provider Selection and Management Policy for details on required processes and provisions for all service providers.

### Incident Management

The bank shall establish procedures for reporting unintended outcomes or system failures. If regulatory risks arise regarding an AI related incident, the compliance and legal departments must be contacted and engaged immediately. Refer to the bank's Incident Response Policy & Plan for detailed information on how to detect and respond to incidents.

### Customer Rights and Communication

When the bank is involved with the use of AI technology, it is important to have full transparency of use with customers. Customers that are affected by AI decisions (e.g. loan approvals) must be:

- Informed of the automated nature of the decision.

- Provided a clear explanation upon request.

- Offered a channel for human review and appeal. Clear documentation must identify the person/s responsible for each AI model.

### Training & Awareness

All employees shall promote a culture of transparency and accountability. Employees working with or impacted by AI technologies must complete annual training on AI ethics and responsible use, data privacy and security, and bias mitigation and fairness principles.

## Compliance and Ongoing Monitoring

Ongoing monitoring will take place for all AI service providers in accordance with the bank's Service Provider Management Policy and through its proactive monitoring and maintenance processes to detect and respond to security threats, vulnerabilities, and anomalies in AI products. AI Model security configurations shall be regularly reviewed and updated to protect against emerging threats, and the bank shall ensure the AI service providers are regularly audited for compliance with internal security policies, regulatory requirements, and industry standards. Any identified vulnerabilities or incidents shall be documented and reported to the ITSC and remediated in a timely manner.
